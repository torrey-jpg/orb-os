# Written from established dental anatomy, history and science. FOR DR. HOLLAR TO REVIEW.
Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in the human body.
Enamel is about 96 percent mineral, mostly a crystal called hydroxyapatite.
Enamel has no living cells, so the body cannot regrow it once it is lost.
Dentin, the layer under the enamel, is filled with microscopic tubules running toward the pulp.
The pulp at the center of a tooth holds its nerves and blood vessels.
Cementum, a thin bone-like layer, covers the roots and anchors the periodontal ligament.
The periodontal ligament acts like a shock absorber between a tooth and its socket.
Adults usually have 32 permanent teeth, including the four wisdom teeth.
Children have 20 primary teeth, often called baby teeth.
The first primary teeth usually appear at around 6 months of age.
Most children have all 20 primary teeth by about age 3.
The first permanent molars arrive around age 6, behind the baby teeth, so they are called six-year molars.
Second molars usually arrive around age 12, earning the name twelve-year molars.
Wisdom teeth usually arrive between ages 17 and 21, if they arrive at all.
Many people never develop one or more of their wisdom teeth.
Lower central incisors are usually the first baby teeth to come in.
The upper canine has the longest root of any tooth in the mouth.
The lower central incisor is the smallest permanent tooth.
Upper first molars usually have three roots; lower molars usually have two.
Canines are sometimes called eye teeth because of their position under the eyes.
Premolars are also called bicuspids, after their two main cusps.
Incisors cut, canines tear, premolars and molars crush and grind.
The Universal Numbering System numbers adult teeth from 1 to 32.
In Universal numbering, tooth number 1 is the upper right third molar.
In Universal numbering, tooth number 8 is the upper right central incisor.
The FDI system, used in much of the world, gives each tooth a two-digit number.
Baby teeth in the Universal system are lettered A through T instead of numbered.
A tooth's crown is the part above the gum; the root is the part below.
The narrow area where crown meets root is called the cervical line, or CEJ.
Teeth are not bones: they cannot repair themselves the way bones do.
Teeth begin forming before birth, while a baby is still in the womb.
The buds for some permanent teeth form before birth too.
Saliva helps neutralize acids and wash away food particles.
Saliva contains calcium and phosphate that help repair early enamel damage.
The body produces saliva all day, with much less flowing during sleep.
Less saliva at night is one reason a bedtime brush matters so much.
Dry mouth raises the risk of cavities because saliva protects the teeth.
Fluoride helps enamel resist acid and can help reverse early decay.
Fluoride works by forming fluorapatite, which is more acid-resistant than enamel crystal.
Community water fluoridation began in Grand Rapids, Michigan, in 1945.
The CDC named community water fluoridation one of the great public health achievements of the 20th century.
Cavities are caused by acids made when mouth bacteria break down sugars.
Streptococcus mutans is one of the bacteria most associated with tooth decay.
Dental plaque is a sticky biofilm of bacteria that forms on teeth every day.
Plaque that is not removed can harden into tartar, also called calculus.
Tartar cannot be removed by brushing; it takes a professional cleaning.
How often you eat sugar matters as much as how much sugar you eat.
Sipping a sugary drink slowly keeps teeth in an acid bath for longer.
Acids in soda, citrus and sports drinks can erode enamel directly.
Waiting a little while to brush after acidic food gives enamel time to recover.
Sugar-free gum stimulates saliva, which helps protect teeth after meals.
Xylitol, a sugar substitute in some gum, is not fermented by cavity-causing bacteria.
Brushing twice a day for two minutes is the standard recommendation.
A soft-bristled toothbrush is recommended for most people.
Replace your toothbrush every three to four months, or sooner if it frays.
Brushing too hard can wear enamel and push gums back.
Cleaning between teeth reaches surfaces a toothbrush cannot.
Gingivitis, the earliest stage of gum disease, is usually reversible.
Periodontitis is advanced gum disease that can destroy the bone around teeth.
Leap day bonus: brushing twice a day in a leap year adds up to 732 brushings.
Bleeding gums when brushing are a common early sign of gingivitis.
Gum disease is a leading cause of tooth loss in adults.
Smoking greatly increases the risk of gum disease.
Research has linked gum disease with heart disease and diabetes.
Dentists can spot signs of some body-wide diseases during a routine exam.
A dental exam includes a check for signs of oral cancer.
Dental X-rays let dentists see decay between teeth and below the gumline.
Bitewing X-rays show the crowns of upper and lower back teeth together.
A panoramic X-ray shows all the teeth and jaws in a single image.
Digital dental X-rays use much less radiation than older film X-rays.
A sealant is a thin coating painted on the chewing surfaces of back teeth.
Sealants help keep food and bacteria out of the deep grooves of molars.
The grooves on molars are called pits and fissures.
Composite fillings are tooth-colored and bond directly to the tooth.
Amalgam fillings have been used for more than 150 years.
A crown is a cap that covers a damaged tooth to restore its shape and strength.
A dental bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring to the teeth beside it.
A root canal removes infected pulp and seals the inside of the tooth.
After a root canal, many back teeth need a crown to protect them.
Dental implants are titanium or zirconia posts that fuse with the jawbone.
The fusion of an implant with bone is called osseointegration.
Swedish surgeon Per-Ingvar Branemark discovered osseointegration in the 1950s.
The first modern titanium dental implant was placed in 1965.
Veneers are thin shells bonded to the front of teeth.
Orthodontic braces move teeth by applying gentle, steady pressure.
Teeth can move because bone reshapes itself around them under pressure.
A retainer keeps teeth from drifting back after braces come off.
A malocclusion is any misalignment of the upper and lower teeth.
An overjet is how far the upper front teeth stick out past the lower ones.
An overbite is how far the upper front teeth overlap the lower ones vertically.
A crossbite is when upper teeth sit inside the lower teeth instead of outside.
Bruxism is the habit of grinding or clenching the teeth.
Many people grind their teeth during sleep without knowing it.
A night guard can protect teeth from grinding damage.
The jaw joint is called the temporomandibular joint, or TMJ.
The masseter is one of the strongest muscles in the body for its size.
Molars handle far more biting force than the front teeth.
Each tooth is unique; even identical twins have different dental details.
Dental records have long been used to help identify people.
Forensic odontology is the study of teeth as evidence.
Paul Revere, the American patriot, also worked as a dentist.
Paul Revere identified the remains of Dr. Joseph Warren by a false tooth he had made for him.
Doc Holliday, the famous Old West gambler, was trained as a dentist.
George Washington lost almost all of his teeth and wore several sets of dentures.
George Washington's dentures were not wood; they used ivory, metal and human and animal teeth.
John Greenwood made dentures for George Washington.
Pierre Fauchard, a French surgeon, is called the father of modern dentistry.
Fauchard published The Surgeon Dentist in 1728, the first complete dental textbook.
The world's first dental college opened in Baltimore in 1840.
The Baltimore College of Dental Surgery is now part of the University of Maryland.
The DDS degree, Doctor of Dental Surgery, was first awarded in Baltimore.
DDS and DMD are equivalent degrees; schools simply choose different names.
Harvard first awarded the DMD degree, Doctor of Dental Medicine, in 1867.
The American Dental Association was founded in 1859.
The first ADA Seal for a dental product was awarded in 1931.
Horace Wells, a dentist, used nitrous oxide for a tooth extraction in 1844.
William Morton, a dentist, demonstrated ether anesthesia in Boston in 1846.
Novocaine, a local anesthetic once common in dentistry, was introduced in 1905.
Lidocaine became widely used in dentistry after its development in the 1940s.
Lucy Hobbs Taylor became the first American woman to earn a dental degree, in 1866.
Robert Tanner Freeman was the first African American to earn a dental degree, in 1869.
Wilhelm Roentgen discovered X-rays in 1895.
Dentists began using X-rays within weeks of their discovery.
The first electric dental drill was patented by George Green in 1875.
The high-speed air-turbine dental drill arrived in the 1950s.
Barbers once pulled teeth as part of their trade.
Ancient Egyptians made a tooth-cleaning powder that included ash and myrrh.
Chew sticks, frayed twigs used to clean teeth, have been used for thousands of years.
The bristle toothbrush was invented in China in the 1400s.
Early bristle toothbrushes used hog bristles on bamboo or bone handles.
Nylon toothbrush bristles were introduced in 1938.
William Addis made one of the first mass-produced toothbrushes in England around 1780.
Toothpaste was first sold in a collapsible tube in the 1890s.
Dental floss was first promoted by New Orleans dentist Levi Spear Parmly in the early 1800s.
The first patent for dental floss was granted in 1874.
Silk was the original material for dental floss.
Nylon floss replaced silk during World War II.
Fluoride toothpaste became widely available in the 1950s.
Crest was the first fluoride toothpaste to earn the ADA Seal, in 1960.
Archaeologists found a 9,000-year-old drilled tooth in Pakistan.
A 6,500-year-old jaw from Slovenia shows a tooth filled with beeswax.
Ancient people sometimes believed tooth decay was caused by tooth worms.
The tooth worm myth lasted for centuries before being disproven.
Ancient Etruscans made gold dental bridges more than 2,500 years ago.
The Maya decorated teeth with inlays of jade and other stones.
Porcelain dentures were developed in France in the late 1700s.
Vulcanite rubber made dentures cheaper and more common in the 1800s.
After the Battle of Waterloo, teeth from fallen soldiers were used in dentures.
Dentures made with teeth from soldiers who fell at Waterloo in 1815 were called Waterloo teeth.
The tooth fairy tradition grew popular in the United States in the 1900s.
In parts of Europe and Latin America, a mouse collects lost baby teeth instead of a fairy.
In Spain and Latin America, the tooth-collecting mouse is called Ratoncito Perez.
In some cultures, children throw lost baby teeth onto the roof for luck.
National Children's Dental Health Month is celebrated every February in the United States.
World Oral Health Day is observed every year on March 20.
National Dentist's Day in the United States is celebrated on March 6.
October is National Dental Hygiene Month in the United States.
The modern dental hygienist profession began in 1913 in Connecticut.
Dr. Alfred Fones trained the first dental hygienists and is called the father of dental hygiene.
Irene Newman, trained by Dr. Alfred Fones, became the first licensed dental hygienist.
Sharks continually replace their teeth throughout their lives.
A shark can grow and lose thousands of teeth in a lifetime.
Elephant tusks are actually long incisor teeth.
Elephants grow six sets of molars in their lifetime.
A narwhal's tusk is a tooth that can grow more than 8 feet long.
The narwhal tusk is a spiral tooth that usually grows from the left side of the jaw.
Rodent incisors never stop growing.
Beavers have orange incisors because their enamel contains iron.
Rabbits' teeth grow continuously, so they must chew to keep them worn down.
Snails have a ribbon-like tongue covered with thousands of tiny teeth.
Giraffes have no upper front teeth; they use a tough pad instead.
Cows have no upper incisors either, only a hard dental pad.
Dogs have 42 adult teeth, ten more than humans.
Cats have 30 adult teeth.
Horses' teeth keep erupting slowly throughout their lives.
You can estimate a horse's age by looking at its teeth.
Judging a horse's age by its teeth gave us the saying: never look a gift horse in the mouth.
Crocodiles can replace each of their teeth many times over a lifetime.
Hippos have huge canine teeth that keep growing throughout life.
Walrus tusks are elongated canine teeth.
Blue whales have no teeth; they filter food through baleen plates.
Baleen is made of keratin, the same protein as fingernails.
Dolphins have one set of teeth for life and do not replace them.
Some dolphins have around 100 conical teeth.
Armadillos have simple peg-like teeth with no enamel.
Anteaters have no teeth at all.
Birds have no teeth; they grind food in a muscular organ called the gizzard.
Platypuses have teeth as babies but lose them and use grinding pads instead.
Mosquitoes do not have teeth; they use a needle-like mouthpart.
Tyrannosaurus rex had serrated teeth that could be about a foot long including the root.
Dinosaurs replaced their teeth throughout their lives.
Mammals typically get only two sets of teeth: baby teeth and adult teeth.
The term diphyodont means having two successive sets of teeth, like humans.
Humans are omnivores, and our mix of tooth shapes reflects that.
Teeth present at birth are called natal teeth.
Teeth that erupt within the first month of life are called neonatal teeth.
An extra tooth is called a supernumerary tooth.
A tooth that is missing from birth is called congenitally missing.
After wisdom teeth, lower second premolars and upper lateral incisors are most often missing.
A tooth that cannot fully erupt into the mouth is called impacted.
Lower wisdom teeth are the most commonly impacted teeth.
Hyperdontia means having extra teeth beyond the normal count.
Hypodontia means having fewer teeth than normal.
A tooth knocked out of its socket is called avulsed.
A knocked-out permanent tooth has the best chance of saving if replanted within an hour.
If a permanent tooth is knocked out, keep it moist, ideally in milk, and see a dentist fast.
Never scrub a knocked-out tooth; handle it by the crown, not the root.
Baby teeth that are knocked out are usually not put back in.
Mouthguards help protect teeth during contact sports.
Mouthguards are required equipment in high school football in the United States.
Baby teeth hold space for the permanent teeth growing beneath them.
Losing a baby tooth too early can let neighboring teeth drift into its space.
Baby teeth have thinner enamel than permanent teeth.
Baby teeth are whiter than permanent teeth, which can look more yellow by comparison.
Baby teeth roots dissolve as the permanent teeth push up beneath them.
Most children begin losing baby teeth around age 6.
Most children lose their last baby tooth around age 12.
A child's first dental visit is recommended by the first birthday.
Early childhood cavities can begin as soon as the first tooth appears.
Putting a baby to bed with a bottle of milk or juice can cause tooth decay.
Tooth decay is one of the most common chronic diseases of childhood.
Dental cavities are among the most common diseases in the world.
Tooth sensitivity often comes from exposed dentin and its open tubules.
Toothpastes for sensitive teeth can work by blocking dentin tubules or calming nerves.
Receding gums can expose root surfaces, which have no enamel.
Root surfaces decay more easily than enamel because they are softer.
A cracked tooth can cause sharp pain when biting or releasing.
An abscess is a pocket of infection at a tooth root or in the gums.
A dental abscess needs professional treatment and will not go away on its own.
Toothaches can sometimes be felt in the ear, jaw or head.
Upper back tooth pain can sometimes come from a sinus infection.
The roots of upper molars sit very close to the maxillary sinus.
Halitosis is the medical term for bad breath.
Much bad breath comes from bacteria on the back of the tongue.
Cleaning your tongue can help reduce bad breath.
The tongue is covered with small bumps called papillae.
Taste buds sit mostly on the papillae of the tongue.
Humans have thousands of taste buds that are replaced every one to two weeks.
The tongue is made of several muscles working together.
Most people have a frenum, a small fold of tissue, under the tongue and inside the lips.
A tight tongue frenum, often called tongue-tie, can affect feeding and speech.
Canker sores are not contagious; cold sores are.
Cold sores are caused by the herpes simplex virus.
The roof of the mouth is called the palate.
The hard palate is bone; the soft palate behind it is muscle.
The uvula is the small fleshy piece hanging at the back of the soft palate.
The lips are red because the skin there is thin and blood vessels show through.
The mouth contains hundreds of different species of bacteria.
Not all mouth bacteria are bad; many help keep the mouth healthy.
Antonie van Leeuwenhoek described bacteria from his own dental plaque in 1683.
G.V. Black, a pioneer dentist, standardized cavity preparation and classification.
G.V. Black's cavity classes, Class I to Class V, are still taught today.
Frederick McKay investigated the brown-stained teeth of Colorado Springs residents.
The brown-stained teeth seen in Colorado Springs were caused by naturally high fluoride in the water.
Dentist Frederick McKay's research helped lead to the discovery that fluoride prevents decay.
H. Trendley Dean's studies set the level of fluoride used in community water.
Mottled enamel from too much fluoride is called dental fluorosis.
Most dental fluorosis is very mild and appears as faint white spots.
Tooth whitening works by using peroxide to break down stains inside enamel.
Whitening does not change the color of fillings, crowns or veneers.
Coffee, tea and red wine are common causes of tooth staining.
Tobacco use is a major cause of both stained teeth and gum disease.
Teeth naturally become a little more yellow with age as enamel thins.
Enamel is translucent, so the yellowish dentin beneath shows through.
The color of your teeth is partly genetic.
Sports drinks can be as hard on enamel as soda because of their acids.
Cheese can help protect teeth by raising mouth pH and adding calcium.
Calcium and vitamin D support strong teeth and the jawbone.
Vitamin C deficiency, called scurvy, causes bleeding gums and loose teeth.
Chewing ice can crack teeth and fillings.
Thumb sucking that continues past early childhood can affect how teeth line up.
Pacifier use is best weaned by around age 2 to 4.
A dry socket is a painful condition after an extraction when the blood clot is lost.
Using a straw right after an extraction can dislodge the healing clot.
Local anesthesia numbs only the area being treated.
Dentists are trained to recognize and help with dental anxiety.
Nitrous oxide, sometimes called laughing gas, is still used in dentistry today.
A dental loupe is a magnifying lens dentists wear to see fine detail.
Intraoral cameras let patients see exactly what the dentist sees.
Many dental offices now use 3D scanners instead of messy impression trays.
3D printers can make dental models, guides and even some restorations.
CAD/CAM systems can mill a crown in the dental office in a single visit.
Lasers are used in some dental procedures on gums and teeth.
The dental mirror lets a dentist see the back of teeth and reflect light.
The explorer is a sharp, pointed instrument used to feel tooth surfaces.
A periodontal probe measures the depth of the pocket between gum and tooth.
Healthy gum pockets are usually about 1 to 3 millimeters deep.
A scaler is used to remove tartar from teeth.
Prophylaxis is the clinical term for a professional dental cleaning.
Most people should have regular dental checkups, often every six months.
There are twelve recognized dental specialties in the United States.
Orthodontics was the first dental specialty recognized in the United States.
Endodontists specialize in the inside of the tooth, including root canals.
Periodontists specialize in the gums and supporting structures of teeth.
Prosthodontists specialize in replacing missing teeth.
Pediatric dentists specialize in caring for children's teeth.
Oral and maxillofacial surgeons treat the mouth, jaws and face.
Edward Angle is known as the father of modern orthodontics.
Edward Angle's system for classifying bite problems is still used by orthodontists today.
Clear aligners straighten teeth using a series of removable plastic trays.
Most dental schools in the United States take four years to complete.
Dental students practice on manikins before treating patients.
The word dentist comes from the Latin word dens, meaning tooth.
The word dentistry entered English in the 1700s.
The word orthodontics comes from Greek words meaning straight teeth.
The word periodontal means around the tooth.
The word endodontic means inside the tooth.
The tooth's chewing surface is called the occlusal surface.
The tooth surface facing the cheek is called the buccal surface.
The tooth surface facing the lips is called the labial surface.
The tooth surface facing the tongue is called the lingual surface.
The side of a tooth toward the midline is mesial; the side away is distal.
Where two teeth touch is called the contact point.
The pointed tips on the chewing surfaces of teeth are called cusps.
Upper first molars often have a small extra cusp called the cusp of Carabelli.
Mamelons are the three small bumps on new incisor edges; they usually wear away with use.
The gum tissue between two teeth is called the interdental papilla.
The jawbone that holds the teeth is called the alveolar bone.
The upper jaw is the maxilla; the lower jaw is the mandible.
The mandible is the strongest bone in the face.
The mandible is the only bone in the skull that moves freely.
When teeth are lost, the jawbone in that area slowly shrinks.
Implants can help preserve jawbone by giving it something to work against.
Modern human jaws are smaller than those of our ancient ancestors.
Smaller jaws help explain why wisdom teeth often lack room to erupt.
Teeth are some of the best-preserved parts of ancient human remains.
Scientists can learn about ancient diets by studying fossil teeth.
Tooth enamel can record clues about where a person grew up.
Enamel forms in growth layers, a little like the rings of a tree.
Microscopic growth lines in enamel are called perikymata on the tooth surface.
A tooth's enamel is thickest on the chewing surfaces of molars.
Enamel can be about 2.5 millimeters thick at the cusps of molars.
Dentin makes up most of a tooth's structure.
Dentin is softer than enamel but harder than bone.
The body can lay down new dentin inside a tooth throughout life.
As we age, the pulp chamber gets smaller as dentin builds up inside.
Odontoblasts are the cells that make dentin.
Ameloblasts are the cells that make enamel, and they are lost once the tooth erupts.
Teeth develop from tissue called the tooth germ.
Tooth development is divided into stages called bud, cap and bell.
Some people are born with a condition called amelogenesis imperfecta, affecting enamel.
Dentinogenesis imperfecta is an inherited condition affecting dentin.
Ectodermal dysplasia can cause missing or unusually shaped teeth.
Peg laterals are small, cone-shaped upper lateral incisors.
Shovel-shaped incisors have raised edges on their tongue side and are common in some populations.
A diastema is a gap between two teeth.
The most common diastema is between the two upper front teeth.
Gold has been used to repair teeth for thousands of years.
Gold is still used in dentistry because it is durable and gentle on opposing teeth.
Zirconia is a very strong white ceramic used for crowns and implants.
Porcelain crowns can be made to match the color of natural teeth closely.
A shade guide helps match a restoration to the color of natural teeth.
Teeth look different under different light, so dentists often check shades in daylight.
Some ancient Romans cleaned their teeth with powders made from bone and shells.
Sugar became widely available in Europe in the 1600s, and tooth decay increased.
Queen Elizabeth I was famous for her blackened teeth from eating sugar.
The first known dental professional, Hesy-Re, practiced in ancient Egypt about 4,600 years ago.
Hesy-Re's tomb inscription called him the chief of dentists and physicians.
Hippocrates wrote about teeth and gum disease more than 2,000 years ago.
The phrase long in the tooth comes from horses, whose gums recede with age.
The phrase by the skin of your teeth comes from the Book of Job.
To cut your teeth on something means to gain your first experience with it.
Something with teeth has real force or power, like a law with teeth.
A wisdom tooth gets its name because it arrives when a person is older and wiser.
A sweet tooth is a fondness for sugary foods.
